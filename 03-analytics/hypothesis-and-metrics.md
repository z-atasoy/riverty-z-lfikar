## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** "I don't need another credit card. I need fewer payment decisions." (UXR-11). Supporting friction: she has to remember which method works where (UXR-09), and sometimes forgets whether she paid with Klarna, PayPal or her bank (UXR-05).
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 78% of Riverty users use 3 or more payment methods, and 62% of their online BNPL purchases go to other providers. Riverty is picked 6% of the time when it appears next to Klarna or PayPal, but 71% when it is the only option. BaFin reports that 14% of BNPL users have lost track of open payments (public), against 27% in our synthetic survey.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Nadine, 38, a frequent online shopper who juggles 3 or more payment methods a month. She wants one consistent way to pay, online and in store, with fewer decisions. The friction we confirmed is that she re-decides at every checkout, depends on whether Riverty is listed, and later can't recall which provider paid.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Nadine has no single payment method that works the same everywhere, so she re-decides at every checkout. Riverty only wins when a merchant lists it, so it never becomes her routine.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Behaviour change: Nadine moves from choosing whatever the shop offers to paying with the Riverty card by default, in store and online. Revenue: interchange on every transaction, plus interest from the roughly 22% who revolve a balance. Retention: a habit lowers inactivity, which our benchmark puts at 25–40% a year against the model's 5%.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The card's share of an active cardholder's monthly payments. The pilot baseline is 14%, and the target is about 20%.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Riverty checkout GMV at partner merchants among cardholders. It must not fall more than 5% against matched non-cardholders.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** 90 days after launch, with at least 500 activated cardholders. That means inviting roughly 3,000 users at the pilot's invited-to-activated rate of 17%.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on Nadine's "I need fewer payment decisions" and data showing that 78% of Riverty users juggle 3 or more payment methods while Riverty is chosen only 6% of the time when it competes at checkout, I believe that giving her one Riverty card that works online and in store, with optional instalments, will make Riverty her default way to pay, as measured by a 40% relative increase in the card's share of her monthly payments (from 14% to about 20%). I will protect Riverty checkout GMV at partner merchants (no more than a 5% decline against matched non-cardholders) and will make a go/no-go decision after 90 days with at least 500 activated cardholders.
