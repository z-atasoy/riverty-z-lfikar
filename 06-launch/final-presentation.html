Slide 5 · Strategy

A moderate threat that a capped test can answer

Problem hook

The user is currently forced to use whichever BNPL provider or payment card is available at checkout because Riverty is only available at selected merchants and is not her preferred payment option.

Value proposition

With the card, user can shop without the concern of Riverty being available in checkout for each merchant and start building some loyalty towards this option carrying it over to physical purchases as well.

If Riverty does nothing for 12 months

Riverty keeps staying dependent on merchant relationships and acquisition to scale up and stays behind in competition for a consumer card.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on Nadine’s “I need fewer payment decisions” and data showing that 78% of Riverty users use 3 or more payment methods while Riverty is chosen only 6% of the time when it competes at checkout, I believe that offering her the option to add her Riverty card to Apple Pay at approval, instead of after the physical card arrives, will make it her default way to pay. Measured by a +4 percentage point change (14% → 18%) in the card’s share of her monthly payments over days 61–90 after approval. I will protect Riverty checkout GMV at partner merchants (no more than 5% below control) and decide at the readout.

Cold read of my own hook
  • Risk: a moderate strategic threat, not a crisis. It justifies a capped test with a cannibalisation guardrail.
  • Misery: systemic as behaviour, mild as pain. Abandonment is 17% vs 8% when the usual method is missing.
  • Value prop: partly. Universal acceptance removes the “is Riverty listed?” obstacle; “more flexible” is generic.

Nadine’s quote (UXR-11): “I don’t need another credit card. I need fewer payment decisions.”

Slide 6 · Research

Her workaround is memory. Riverty loses by default, not by comparison.

Persona · workaround today

Nadine, 38, frequent online shopper who juggles three or more payment methods every month. Goal: one consistent way to pay that feels the same online and in a store, with fewer payment decisions.

External tool

Relies on memory of which method works at which merchant.

The process
  1. Check what she will buy
  2. Think which methods are available in which merchants
  3. Find the merchants and method matching what she will buy
Core frustration

She cannot memorize or keep in mind each and every merchant and payment method combo, so she sometimes misses opportunities.

Evidence (UXR-09): “having to remember which one works where”.

Competitive landscape (from the case)
Klarna CardDirect benchmark; own banking licence and scheme membership
PayPalCards plus Pay in 3 / Pay in 30; the brand most Riverty users already carry
Instalment card banksSantander Zero, Advanzia, TF Bank already hold the interest pool
Scalapay · FLOA · AlmaTake BNPL into stores, or pair instalments with their own card
Do nothingTap whatever the checkout offers, or use an existing card
Moment of misery

Standing in a store or at a checkout, she has to remember which method works where, and later may not recall whether she paid with Klarna, PayPal or her bank.

PRD highlights · C1 Wallet-Ready Instant Card
  • Vision: every Riverty customer carries a Riverty card in her phone wallet the moment she’s approved.
  • Scope: 10 Must-haves: 4 core, 4 enabling (SCA, authorisation and fraud rules, freeze, logging), 2 supporting. Apple Pay first; Google Pay is a Should (a Must if Android is above about 30%).
  • Three screens: “You’re approved” → “Adding your card” → “Your card is ready”.
  • Made explicit: success is share of monthly payments (14% → 18%), paired with a partner-merchant GMV guardrail. My synthetic worst case loses about EUR 11 per card a year.
Overrides and what the prototype taught me
  • Override: C4 moved from Time Sinker to Major Project (value 3 → 4). Riverty already runs Flex, so integration effort is lower.
  • Gap: “Try again keeps progress” was undefined; I made it skip the identity step.
  • Gap: the 3-tap target counted “done”, but success appears automatically, so the happy path is 2 taps.
  • Gap: several Musts (real-time authorisation, fraud rules) can’t be prototyped.

Slide 8 · Validation

One change, one primary metric, a decision rule I can be held to

Control (A)

“You’re approved” with the line “Your physical card is on its way. You can add it to Apple Pay once it arrives.” Virtual card live for online use; details behind biometrics; Freeze toggle. She can’t pay in a shop from her phone until the plastic arrives.

Variant (B) · exactly one change

Add to Apple Pay is offered at approval, instead of after the physical card arrives. “Pay with Riverty in any store, from your phone” → Adding your card → Your card is ready.

Primary metric

Card’s share of an approved user’s monthly payments (card payments ÷ all her payments in the month), days 61–90 after approval.

14%baseline (synthetic, 69 users)
+4 ppMDE (14% → 18%)
1,318evaluable per arm (about 2,900 approvals)
α 0.05two-sided · 80% power · 50/50

Duration about 30 weeks at a 40% iPhone share (22 if all users are on iPhone).

Guardrails
  • Riverty checkout GMV at partner merchants per cardholder, treatment vs control: no more than 5% below, over a rolling 28 days.
  • 30+ day arrears: 1.4% baseline, 2.0% ceiling.
  • Revenue per cardholder: not below control.
Held constant

KYC and approval, Mastercard and terms, SCA, fraud rules, freeze, the wallet-add flow itself (A reaches it later), no nudges or promotions, same app build.

ShipLift ≥ +4 pp at p < 0.05, the GMV guardrail passes, arrears ≤ 2.0%, revenue per cardholder ≥ control.
IterateLift between +1.5 and +4 pp, or the guardrail is inconclusive: extend enrolment or rework.
KillUpper bound of the 95% CI below +2 pp at full sample, or arrears above 2.0%.
PauseGMV more than 5% below control in 2 consecutive weekly reads (1,000+ users per arm): pause enrolment into B.

The read date is fixed once the last enrolled user reaches day 90. No early stopping on the primary metric.

Slide 10 · Story

What pushed back, and what I’d do next

Friction points
  • The hook overstated it. “Forced” isn’t coercion; users are passive. A cold read downgraded it to a moderate threat.
  • The prototype exposed PRD gaps: retry semantics, the 3-tap target, lockout scope and offline timing.
  • The experiment had traps: measuring “active” users after the change would bias the metric, and the GMV guardrail is underpowered at 2,900 per arm.
  • The launch plan contradicted the test: “identical copy in both arms” was impossible, and the timeline didn’t match the sample size.
Aha moment

The metric and the guardrail decide what you build. A card can win usage while moving volume from a roughly 2.9% merchant fee to about 0.3% interchange. In my synthetic worst case that loses about EUR 11 per card a year.

Key takeaways · what I would do next
  1. Confirm approval happens inside the app, and the real iPhone share. Every week number depends on them.
  2. Decide on Google Pay: a Must if Android is above about 30% of the cohort.
  3. Run the prototype gate, register the analysis plan, and check Apple entitlement lead times.
  4. Have Finance restate the year-1 numbers: about 9,000 exposed users, not 31,238 cards.
  5. Decide with the Head of Cards whether to enrol 2,900 or 4,900 per arm, which moves the readout by about 10 weeks.
  6. After the readout: the “Not now” reminder and the default-card nudge (C3).
